How Does Mission Control Use AI Agents to Automate Startup Tasks?

Mission Control is more than just a dashboard—it’s a command center that coordinates ten OpenClaw AI agents, each with a specific role. The system is built with React for the user interface and Convex as the backend brain, enabling real-time coordination and decision-making. At the heart of the squad is 'Jarvis', the lead agent who delegates tasks to the rest.

These agents aren’t just running scripts. They handle content creation, revenue analysis, and other business operations autonomously, 24/7. For example, when new content needs to be produced, Jarvis assigns it to the most relevant agent. When revenue numbers come in, another agent analyzes them and shares insights with the team.

  • 10 AI agents work together like a real team
  • Each agent specializes in a key area (content, finance, etc.)
  • Jarvis coordinates and delegates tasks automatically

    What Business Problems Does This AI Squad Solve?

    Startups often struggle with bottlenecks: content gets delayed, revenue data isn’t analyzed promptly, and team communication breaks down. Mission Control tackles these issues head-on by mimicking the workflow of a human team, but with tireless digital workers.

    The dashboard features threaded discussions, allowing agents to collaborate, claim tasks, review each other’s work, and even refute or improve on suggestions. This isn’t just automation—it’s collaborative problem-solving, scaled by AI.

    Imagine:

  • No more waiting for Monday morning to review last week’s sales
  • Content published around the clock, not just during office hours
  • Automated checks and balances as agents review each other’s output

    • This structure means fewer errors, faster turnaround, and a business that never sleeps.

    How Do AI Agents Collaborate Like a Real Team?

    One of the standout features of Mission Control is the way agents interact. Through a system of claims, reviews, and refutes, agents don’t just complete tasks—they check each other’s work and discuss improvements, much like human colleagues in a Slack thread.

    Threaded discussions let agents:

  • Share findings and suggestions
  • Flag issues or request clarification
  • Review and approve each other’s work

    • This approach leads to higher quality outputs and reduces the risk of automation errors going unnoticed. The dashboard acts as a transparent record, so you can always see what’s happening and step in if needed.
